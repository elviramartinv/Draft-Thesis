\chapter{The \texorpdfstring{$\tau_h$}{τh} identification algorithms and their performance}
\label{ch:Tau}

\noindent The precise and accurate reconstruction of $\tau$ leptons is a crucial part of the $HH\rightarrow bb\tau\tau$ analysis covered in Chapter \ref{ch:bbtt_analysis}. Furthermore, a significant number of physical analyses — such as measurements of Higgs boson production~\cite{CMS-HIG-16-043,CMS-HIG-19-010} and of the \CP structure of its Yukawa coupling to $\tau$ leptons~\cite{CMS-HIG-20-006}, and searches for new physics in final states with $\tau$ leptons ~\cite{CMS-HIG-21-001,CMS-PAS-EXO-24-012,CMS-PAS-EXO-24-020} — rely on the adequate identification of $\tau$ decay products and their properties. This broad physics program necessitates continuous development of reconstruction and identification techniques, which in CMS for $\tau$ leptons are coordinated within the Tau Physics Object Group (Tau POG). A central component of the study of $\tau$ leptons, which will be emphasized subsequently, involves validating the applied techniques to ensure their correct behavior in data.

In CMS, the reconstruction and identification of hadronic $\tau$ leptons constitute two complementary steps. The reconstruction stage, performed by the HPS algorithm~\cite{CMS-TAU-14-001} (see Section \ref{subsec:Taus}), assembles the visible decay products into a $\tau_h$ candidate and determines its decay mode and kinematic properties. The identification step then classifies this reconstructed candidates as a genuine $\tau$ lepton or as a misidentified jet, electron or muon. Since Run-2, this classification has been performed using the \textsc{DeepTau} algorithm~\cite{CMS-TAU-20-001}, a convolutional neural network (CNN) that combines information from all individual reconstructed particles near the $\tau_h$ axis with properties of the $\tau_h$ candidate. Recent developments in Run-3 include the updated \textsc{DeepTau v2.5} algorithm, as well as alternative approaches to identify $\tau_h$, such as \textsc{PNet} and \textsc{UParT}, which aim to unify jet flavor tagging and $\tau$ identification within a common framework.

This chapter provides a detailed examination of the identification of hadronic $\tau$ leptons. Section \ref{sec:Tau_MisID} lays the groundwork and emphasizes the challenges associated with distinguishing genuine $\tau_h$ candidates from background objects. Section \ref{sec:DeepTau2.5} details the $\tau_h$ identification algorithm deployed for the early Run-3 data-taking period (\textsc{DeepTau v2.5}), and covers alternative identification strategies currently under study. Finally, in Section \ref{sec:Tau_validation}, the performance of the presented techniques is validated using Run-3 data. 


\section{Challenges in \texorpdfstring{$\tau_h$}{τh} identification}
\label{sec:Tau_MisID}
%
As described in Section \ref{subsec:Taus}, the HPS algorithm is configured to prioritize the reconstruction efficiency of genuine $\tau_h$, which reaches values of around $90\%$~\cite{CMS-TAU-16-003}. Therefore, it inevitably suffers from contamination by other processes, meaning that a fraction of reconstructed $\tau_h$ candidates originates from objects that mimic the topology of hadronic $\tau$ decay. The dominant source of misidentification arises from jets, since the HPS algorithm is seeded by PF jets that can reproduce the charged-track multiplicity and electromagnetic signature characteristic of $\tau$ decays. In essence, a highly collimated quark or gluon jet can resemble a $\tau$ decay, as illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:tau_jet_sim}. Light leptons can also be misidentified as $\tau$ leptons: muons can produce a similar signature to that of a one-prong $\tau$ decay in cases where they deposit significant energy in the calorimeters; while electrons can occasionally produce electromagnetic showers that reach the HCAL, mimicking the signature of a charged pion, and emit photons via bremsstrahlung radiation, which can be reconstructed by the HPS algorithm as strips. An example of these signatures is shown in Figure \ref{fig:ele_fake_tau}.

The performance of the HPS algorithm is also affected by intrinsic limitations in the reconstruction of the $\tau_h$ decay itself. Even for genuine $\tau$ leptons, the algorithm does not always correctly assign the decay mode, resulting in misreconstruction rates of around 11\% for one-prong decays and up to 25\% for one-prong + $\pi^0$ topologies~\cite{CMS-TAU-16-003}. Furthermore, the accuracy of the charge assignment depends on the completeness of the reconstructed final state: in the absence of missing charged hadrons, the charge is correctly determined in about 99\% of cases, while this value decreases to around 70\% for decay modes with missing charged hadrons. 



These effects reflect the challenges associated with the complex topology of hadronic $\tau$ decays, and highlight the need for a dedicated identification stage to improve the purity of reconstructed $\tau_h$ candidates. To this end, various methods have been implemented in CMS over the years, ranging from cut-based isolation sum discriminators and Boosted Decision Tree discriminator~\cite{CMS-TAU-14-001,CMS-TAU-16-003} to more sophisticated deep learning-based algorithms, such as the aforementioned \textsc{DeepTau}~\cite{CMS-TAU-20-001}, as well as alternative tagging algorithms, like ParticleNet \textsc{PNet} \textcolor{red}{ref} and Unified Particle Transformer \textsc{UPartT} \textcolor{red}{ref}. The following section provides a detailed overview of the various ML-based identification algorithms deployed in the CMS experiment.



\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\tau_h$}{τh} identification algorithms}
\label{sec:DeepTau2.5}

\noindent Although the HPS algorithm provides an efficient reconstruction of hadronic $\tau$ candidates, it has been established that it faces intrinsic limitations, such as significant contamination from non-$\tau$ objects and misreconstruction of decay modes. Therefore, a dedicated identification approach is imperative to enhance the purity of the selected $\tau_h$ candidates. The challenge of discriminating between these candidates, given the large number of correlated observables involved in this type of task, has led to the adoption of advanced deep learning techniques capable of fully exploiting the available information and ensure robust performance across different data-taking conditions. The continuous development at CMS has therefore resulted in increasingly sophisticated identification algorithms that significantly surpass the initial approaches adopted during the first two LHC runs.

This has led to the deployment of the \textsc{DeepTau} neural network-based algorithm at the end of Run-2, which has since become the standard $\tau_h$ identification method in CMS. The first version v2.1 \textcolor{red}{ref} was presented initially, and was then upgrade to v2.5 \textcolor{red}{ref} during the early Run-3 period, introducing major improvements that will be discussed next. In parallel, alternative identification strategies for genuine $\tau_h$ candidates are being explored, leveraging modern architectures such as graph neural networks (\textsc{PNet}) and transformers (\textsc{UParT}) to treat $\tau_h$ candidates within a broader jet classification framework.


\subsection{The \textsc{DeepTau} algorithm}

\noindent The \textsc{DeepTau}~\cite{CMS-TAU-20-001} algorithm based on a convolutional deep neural network is designed to simultaneously discriminate hadronically decaying $\tau$ leptons from quark and gluon jets, electrons, and muons. As has already been noted, its primary goal is to enhance the purity of reconstructed $\tau_h$ candidates, addressing the inherent challenges posed by the highly collimated topology of hadronic $\tau$ decays and their similarity to narrow jets and electromagnetic objects, as illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:tau_fakes}.

In order to achieve an optimal $\tau$ identification performance, the algorithm combines high-level reconstructed $\tau$ features together with low-level inputs from the inner tracker, calorimeters and muon sub-detectors using particle flow candidates, electrons and muons reconstructed within the vicinity of the $\tau$ candidate. The high-level block includes variables used during $\tau$ reconstruction or otherwise proven to provide discriminating power. The low-level block describes each object reconstructed within concentric geometric windows around the $\tau_h$ axis through its four-momentum, track quality, compatibility with the primary vertex (PV), as well as characteristics of energy deposits in the detector (ECAL, HCAL and muon station hits), among others. A summary of the input features is given in Table \ref{tab:deeptau_inputs}.

Two versions of the \textsc{DeepTau} algorithm have been used in CMS during Run-2 and Run-3. Figure \ref{fig:DeepTau_discriminator} shows results corresponding to the first version, developed at the end of Run-2 and labeled~\textsc{v2.1}. However, at the start of Run-3, a new iteration was released, upgrading the algorithm considerably. This latest version, \textsc{v2.5}, benefits from an updated training strategy that was optimized in terms of dataset preparation and hyperparameter optimization, resulting in improved classification performance compared to its predecessor.

Among the improvements implemented, the most impactful update to the \textsc{v2.5} model is the incorporation of domain adaptation techniques, developed within the Tau POG~\cite{CMS-TAU-24-001}. These techniques are designed to guarantee that the \textsc{DeepTau} performance remains unaffected by any potential mismodeling effect present in the Monte Carlo simulation utilized for training. The network is therefore trained on a mixture of data and simulation, allowing for the reduction of performance discrepancies between simulation and collision data, which became more pronounced under the evolving detector conditions of Run-3. This results in enhanced simulation-to-data agreement for the \textsc{DeepTau} classifiers, as demonstrated in Figure \ref{fig:domain_adapt}, which depicts the distribution of the \textsc{DeepTauVSjet} discriminator before and after applying domain adaptation. The inclusion of domain adaptation in the agreement has yielded notable improvements, most notably in the final bins.

In summary, the use and continuous development of deep-learning algorithms has proven essential for the challenging task of identifying $\tau_h$ leptons. These algorithms effectively complement the HPS reconstruction, yielding highly satisfactory results in background rejection. \subsection{Alternative strategies for \texorpdfstring{$\tau_h$}{τh} identification}
\label{subsec:PNet}
\noindent %The excellent performance delivered by the \textsc{DeepTau} algorithm is a compelling example of the benefits of advances in machine learning for object identification. In view of the expanding development of innovative techniques that are supported by increasingly sophisticated architectures, the CMS experiment has achieve notable advancements in the identification of hadronically decaying $\tau$. 
In recent years, new taggers based on modern architectures have emerged as part of a \textit{unified jet tagging} philosophy. The concept underlying this approach involves the simultaneous classification of the origin of the jet, whereby the $\tau$ candidate is treated as a jet, integrating the challenge of genuine $\tau_h$ identification with the classification of jet flavor. This strategy offers several benefits, including enhanced management of correlations between the identification of different objects and rejecting specific sources of contamination, as well as optimized use of computing resources through the utilization of a single, versatile algorithm, both in offline reconstruction and in the high-level trigger.

In this context of jet flavor classification, two new algorithms developed in recent years are particularly noteworthy: ParticleNet \textsc{PNet} \textcolor{red}{ref} and Unified Particle Transformer, \textsc{UParT} \textcolor{red}{ref}, each one based on a different architecture.


\subsubsection{The ParticleNet algorithm}

\textsc{PNet} is one of the most notable developments within the unified jet tagging strategy at CMS. This architecture, first introduced in 2019, is based on a representation of the jet as an unordered, permutation-invariance set of particles — entities distributed irregularly in space. This representation of the jet is known as a particle cloud, and it provides a natural way to treat jets, where each point in the cloud corresponds to a PF candidate. This concept is distinct from previous representations of the jet as images in the $\eta-\phi$ plane — the approach followed by \textsc{DeepTau} — or as lists of its constituent particles, as in \textsc{DeepJet} \textcolor{red}{asegurarme de que lo haya introducido}. In regard to this, the particle cloud model suggests that the particles are not unrelated entities but rather are correlated, thus allowing for the capture of the internal structure of the jet.

In contrast to \textsc{DeepTau}, whose input variables rely on the intermediate reconstruction performed by HPS, \textsc{PNet} takes the PF candidates themselves as input, using their kinematics and properties without any prior $\tau_h$-specific reconstruction step. Furthermore, the set of input variables depends on the specific tagging task. To fully exploit the potential of this approach to understanding jets, graph neural networks (GNNs) are employed; this family of architectures is particularly well-suited for working directly with sets of particles that lack a fixed geometric structure and exhibit permutational symmetry. Finally, \textsc{PNet} returns the probability of belonging to each class, also assigning each a working point, similar to how \textsc{DeepTau} operates. In the specific problem of $\tau_h$ identification, the classes are distinguished as hadronic $\tau$, quark or gluon jet, electron, or muon.

%At the time of its introduction, ParticleNet achieved state-of-the-art performance and significantly surpassed existing methods. Its use has become widely adopted in Run-3 analysis for classifying the different jet flavors as a part of the unified jet tagging framework.

At the time of its introduction, \textsc{PNet} achieved state-of-the-art performance in jet flavor tagging, significantly surpassing the methods available at the time~\cite{PNet}. It has since been widely adopted in Run-3 analyses as part of the unified jet tagging framework, primarily for the identification of jets originating from b and c quarks. Its application to $\tau_h$ identification is a more recent addition, whose performance relative
to the established HPS+\textsc{DeepTau} approach is assessed in Section~\ref{subsec:tau_IDcomparison}.


\subsubsection{The Unified Particle Transformer algorithm}

Unified Particle Transformer, \textsc{UParT}, is another recent CMS development for jet classification. The first version, \textsc{v1}, was introduced in \textcolor{red}{202X}, and an updated version featuring architectural refinements and a larger training dataset, \textsc{v2}, was released in 2025. Similar to ParticleNet, \textsc{UParT} is equipped with a unified tagger for jet-based flavor classification where the jet representation is also expressed in terms of particle-clouds. However, this approach employs a transformer-based architecture \textcolor{red}{ref}, along with innovative training strategies, to ensure a comprehensive capture of the correlations between the jet constituents using self-attention mechanisms.

Like \textsc{PNet}, \textsc{UParT} represents the jet as a particle cloud built from PF candidates and is trained on low-level inputs. The difference lies in how the relations between constituents are modeled: whereas the graph-based approach of \textsc{PNet} aggregates information from the nearest neighbors of each particle, the self-attention mechanism of the transformer relates all pairs of constituents simultaneously, providing a global description of the jet. Additionally, the \textsc{UParT} algorithm introduces variables that characterize the interaction between jet constituents and secondary vertices, known as pairwise interaction features, enhancing the sensitivity to internal jet structure.

%Thus, as a particle-cloud tagger, the algorithm uses information directly from the PF candidates — which constitute the elements of the cloud — and is trained using low-level inputs and their correlations. Additionally, the \textsc{UParT} algorithm introduces variables that characterize the interaction between jet constituents and secondary vertices, known as pairwise interaction features, enhancing the sensitivity to internal jet structure. The transformer architecture provides greater flexibility for capturing global patterns, thereby complementing the description based on particle interactions characteristic of graph-based approaches such as~\textsc{PNet}.


In the unified jet tagging framework, the algorithm outputs the probabilities associated with each class considered during training, including hadronic $\tau$ leptons, quark or gluon jets, electrons and muons. The resulting \textsc{UParT} represents a new state-of-the-art approach, demonstrating improved performance with respect to previous models such as \textsc{PNet} in jet flavor classification tasks.

\smallskip
\noindent Two alternative strategies for identifying genuine $\tau_h$ deployed during Run-3 at CMS have been presented in the text above. Both algorithms have been demonstrated to be capable of processing information about the constituents of the jets and discerning non-trivial relations between them. Although both algorithms were originally designed and customized for AK4 jet classification, they both perform in an inclusive manner heavy flavor and hadronic $\tau$ identification combined with a flavor-aware jet energy and jet resolution regression \textcolor{red}{ref}. 

In this context, the $\tau_h$ is treated as simply another jet, and the network simultaneously learns to distinguish it from the other jet flavors. This approach to the problem enables consistent evaluation of correlations across classes while providing a flexible classifier able to directly exploit low-level information without relying on specific object reconstructions, such as those used by HPS or \textsc{DeepTau}.

The application of the aforementioned strategies to the challenge of $\tau_h$ identification enables the assessment of the extent to which these techniques can offer performance that is competitive with or complementary to that of \textsc{DeepTau}, as discussed further in the following Section \ref{subsec:tau_IDcomparison}. \subsection{Comparison between \texorpdfstring{$\tau_h$}{τh} identification algorithms}
\label{subsec:tau_IDcomparison}
%DP2025-073 
To understand the complementarity of the described algorithms for identifying hadronic $\tau$ leptons based on advanced ML techniques, the subsequent section presents a comparison of the performance of each of them: \textsc{DeepTau v2.5}, \textsc{PNet} and \textsc{UParT v2}. The comparison will be made in terms of efficiency in the reconstruction and identification of genuine $\tau_h$ versus the ability to reject misidentified objects, as well as the accuracy in classifying $\tau$ decay modes, with the aim of discerning the strenghts of each algorithm and understanding how they complement one another. 

As previously discussed, a key distinction between the algorithms lies in the neural network architectures they exploit to perform the identification. However, this task requires the use of different reconstructed objects as input for each respective algorithm. To ensure a valid comparison, it is essential to consider the distinct reconstructed objects on which each algorithm operates. As indicated previously, \textsc{DeepTau} operates directly on HPS $\tau_h$ candidates; \textsc{PNet} and \textsc{UParT}, on the other hand, are fed AK4 jets that have been reconstructed by CMS taking into account the subtraction of pileup particles using dedicated algorithms, described in Section \ref{subsec:Jets}: charged-hadron substraction (CHS) and pileup-per-particle identification (PUPPI). Therefore, given the varying reconstruction efficiencies of these objects, a unified collection of $\tau$ candidates is defined, obtained as the union of HPS $\tau$ candidates, CHS jets, and PUPPI jets, to which a very loose selection is applied.

Accordingly, the performance of the classifiers is evaluated by taking into account the reconstruction efficiency of their associated reconstruction algorithm. This evaluation is based on ROC curves that show the fraction of no-$\tau_h$ objects that pass the discriminant (misidentification probability) compared to the fraction of genuine $\tau_h$ from the unified collection that pass the discriminant (efficiency) for each individual discriminant: \textsc{VSjet}, \textsc{VSe} and \textsc{VSmu}. This is depicted in Figure \ref{fig:ROC_tauID}, which demonstrates the performance of each discriminant for quark and gluon jets, electrons, and muons misidentified as $\tau_h$ using HPS+\textsc{DeepTau} (blue in the figure), CHS+\textsc{PNet} (yellow), PUPPI+\textsc{PNet} (red), and PUPPI+\textsc{UParT} (purple), where the solid markers indicate the respective working points. As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:ROC_tauID}, the intrinsic efficiency of the HPS reconstruction is approximately \textcolor{red}{70\%}, and it can be easily recovered using \textsc{PNet}/\textsc{UParT}. The ROC curves also yield different conclusions depending on the discriminant evaluated. Thus, for discrimination agains quark and gluon jets, CHS+\textsc{PNet} demonstrates the most optimal global performance, with HPS+\textsc{DeepTau} offering a close second in the high-purity regime, configuration frequently employed in analyses; whereas in discrimination against electrons and muons, \textsc{UParT} emerges as the leader in performance, with HPS+\textsc{DeepTau} ranking second in the high-purity regime.

UParT achieves the best performance, followed by HPS+DeepTau in high-rejection mode, where its efficiency is not limited by the inefficiency of the HPS reconstruction.

The comparison must be read along two different axes. At a fixed efficiency within the
range accessible to all algorithms, the performances are comparable: CHS+\textsc{PNet}
provides the lowest jet misidentification rate, reducing it by 15--25\% with respect to
HPS+\textsc{DeepTau}, whereas PUPPI+\textsc{UParT} performs worse than
HPS+\textsc{DeepTau} against jets and clearly better against electrons and muons. The
qualitative difference appears instead in the accessible range: HPS+\textsc{DeepTau}
saturates at a $\tau_h$ efficiency of about 70\%, set by the HPS reconstruction itself,
while the jet-based taggers extend up to approximately 90\%, recovering candidates that HPS
does not reconstruct at all.

\section{Performance with \texorpdfstring{$\sqrt{13.6}$}{√13.6} TeV data}
\label{sec:Tau_validation}

Validating the performance of the algorithms presented for reconstructing and identifying $\tau_h$ in real data is an essential step in ensuring the reliability of any physics analysis using hadronic $\tau$ particles. Therefore, although the algorithms detailed in the previous section have been extensively optimized and evaluated in simulations, it is imperative to verify their performance in each data run conducted by CMS, and, by doing so, monitor the behavior of the techniques developed and actually used in analyses under real experimental conditions.

As part of this thesis work, the $\tau_h$ reconstruction and identification performance of these algorithms has been systematically validated within the Tau POG by evaluating data from all years of Run-3, covering the data-taking periods from 2022 through early 2026. The following sections present two fundamental aspects of this validation. First, Section \ref{subsec:Data-MC-agreement} presents the results in terms of Data to MC comparison for all the aforementioned data-taking periods, which resulted in an internal CMS Detector Performance note \textcolor{red}{DP-2025-074}. Second, Section \ref{subsec:TES_checks} addresses the stability and consistency of performance across the different years of data collection and, consequently, across the changing conditions of the detector. \textcolor{red}{invertir el orden? hablar primero de stability de los datos y luego data-to-MC?}


\subsection{Data-to-MC comparison in Run-3}
\label{subsec:Data-MC-agreement}

To evaluate the performance of the $\tau_h$ modeling in CMS, the simulation is compared with data collected during different data-taking periods for various distributions of relevant variables. The simulation utilized for the comparison is based on the Summer24 NanoAODv15 campaign, which serves as the most up-to-date reference, incorporating the variables required for the recent \textsc{PNet} and \textsc{UParT v2} models. The Monte Carlo (MC) simulation is validated using data from all years of Run-3 processed (or reprocessed in the case of data collected during 2022 and 2023) within the same NanoAODv15 campaign, ensuring consistency between the comparison. 

The simulated samples include the main processes contributing to the selected final state: Drell-Yan, top quark pair production, W+jets, and di-Boson processes. The simulation is partially calibrated to describe the data, including corrections unrelated to the $\tau$ particles, such as calibration of the number of pileup interactions, corrections to the top-antitop system momentum and $Z$ boson transverse momentum spectra, and correction factors for muon identification and isolation efficiency. Multijet QCD contribution, which is challenging to model in simulation, is estimated directly from the data in a sideband region adjacent to that used in the validation, and is extrapolated to the signal region.

\subsection{Stability of \texorpdfstring{$\tau_h$}{τh} performance in Run-3 data}
\label{subsec:TES_checks}
%presentación PPD (1): https://indico.cern.ch/event/1569864/contributions/6612866/attachments/3109869/5512492/TauPOG_2024_2025Data_PPD_2407.pdf
%presentación PPD (2): https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/178-DORdl0GOepSjdjDzxOgDB1sgRS6_t_pinHd4K7ro/edit?slide=id.g3c670cd6792_0_1#slide=id.g3c670cd6792_0_1

The previous section presented the Data-to-MC validation, which was designed to verify the correct modeling of $\tau_h$ reconstruction and identification in simulation. This section, in contrast, focuses on evaluating the actual response of the detector throughout the various periods of Run-3. Across these periods, the detector is subject to variations in its operating conditions due to increases in instantaneous luminosity and the aging of the components, which can influence key aspects of $\tau_h$ performance, such as the energy scale, identification efficiency and misidentification rate. Therefore, studying the stability of the collected data becomes imperative to ensure a reliable description of the data through Monte Carlo simulation. These studies thus serve as an independent verification process that complements simulation-based validation. 

The behavior of the data over the years is now addressed using an approach similar to the one presented in previous section. Events from the $Z\to\tau\tau\to\mu\tau_h$ process are selected using data collected with muon triggers and under a preselection criteria analogous to that in Section \ref{subsec:Data-MC-agreement}, with the exception that the $\tau$ must satisfy Tight \textsc{VSjet} WP criterion in order to maximize the purity in genuine $\tau_h$. This is done to ensure that consistency studies actually confirm that any variations across data-taking periods reflect genuine detector effects rather than changes in the background composition. For the studies outlined here, the \textsc{DeepTau} algorithm will be the sole discriminant employed. Furthermore, the cut on the transverse mass $m_T(\mu,MET) < 65$ GeV, as defined in Figure \ref{fig:mT_cut_baseline}, is maintained to preserve a region rich in genuine $\tau_h$ particles.

Figure \ref{figr:data_across_years} shows a comparison of the visible mass of the system $m_{vis}$ over the different periods of Run-3, from 2022 to 2026. Generally, a stable trend is observed across the different data-taking years, with a slight shift observed starting from 2024 onward \textcolor{red}{????}. While deviations of this nature are consistent with the impact of deteriorating detector conditions, they warrant a more detailed investigation of the stability of the data.

To provide a more detailed characterization of these effects, the visible mass of the $\mu\tau_h$ system in the region enriched with Drell-Yan events is studied. As illustrated in \ref{fig:ratio_plots}, this in-depth study provides valuable insights. The strategy employed consists of plotting the ratio of the $m_{vis}$ distribution across different years for a common reference period that varies for each panel, as explained in more detail in the figure caption. This approach serves to illuminate the distinctions between the various data sets. As a result, different behaviors can be observed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The $m_{vis}$ region between $40 < m_{vis} < 90$ GeV corresponds to the $Z\to\tau\tau\to\mu\tau_h$ peak and is therefore dominated by genuine hadronic $\tau$ events. This region exhibits consistent behavior over the years, following a flat ratio in all cases, with a minor decline in efficiency towards the end of Run-3, indicating stability in the response of the detector to genuine $\tau_h$.
    \item Outside the $Z\to\tau\tau\to\mu\tau_h$ peak, in the tails of the $m_{vis}$ spectrum, the distribution is dominated by fake $\tau$ particles, primary from jets misidentified as $\tau_h$ in the W+jets background contribution. In these regions, a slight increase in events is observed for 2024, which becomes particularly noticeable in 2025 and 2026, highlighting the greater sensitivty to fakes.
\end{itemize}

In this way, stability across periods has been verified to account for possible changes in the relative $\tau$ efficiencies, fake rates, and energy scale over the four considered eras. It has been observed that the regions most affected by the different data-acquisition conditions correspond to those dominated by fake $\tau_h$, suchs as events outside the $Z$ mass peak in the DY-enriched region. This indicates that the observed trends stem from variations in the composition and reconstruction of jets misidentified as $\tau_h$ particles, due to detector degradation. Conversely, the response of the detector to authentic hadronic $\tau$ particles remains consistent, validating the stability of the $\tau_h$ energy scale and the efficacy of the reconstruction process throughout the Run-3.

Overall, these stability tests confirm that the performance of the $\tau_h$ in data collected by the detector remains under control across Run-3, with minor variations consistent with the evolution of the detector. This provides an important cross-check of the robustness of the Run-3 $\tau_h$ program.

\noindent This chapter has demonstrated how the HPS reconstruction and the various identification algorithms — \textsc{DeepTau v2.5}, \textsc{PNet} and \textsc{UParT} — enable the successful identification of the hadronic $\tau$ lepton in experimental collision processes. The focus has been on the various strategies available in CMS, and how the evolution of advanced machine learning techniques, as well as the combination of several of them, enables optimal results to be achieved in discriminating hadronic $\tau$ leptons from various sources of misidentification, while maintaining efficiency for genuine $\tau$ leptons. In addition, the thorough validation process using Run-3 data against Monte Carlo simulations demonstrates the resilience of these algorithms performance under actual detector operating conditions, ensuring reliable and consistent modeling across multiple years. The robustness and confidence provided by the $\tau_h$ program are of great importance for complex physical analyses in final states involving $\tau$ leptons. These elements constitute a fundamental pillar for the study of the $HH\to bb\tau\tau$ channel, which will be discussed in the following chapters.
